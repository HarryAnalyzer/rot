An untouched balance of $ROT loses 2% of itself every day. Everything that rots is paid out to the holders who stamped their tokens that week. Stop participating and your balance quietly drains into the pockets of the people who did.

In 1932 the Austrian town of Wörgl issued money that lost value unless the holder bought a stamp and stuck it to the note. Hoarding became expensive, the notes changed hands fast, and a town with 30% unemployment built a bridge. Economists call it demurrage. Austria's central bank banned it in 1933. This is that, as a memecoin, with the stamp enforced by a contract instead of a town clerk.

The token

Epochs

Rot that happens during an epoch is split between the holders who stamped before it started. That is why weight is always locked in before the compost arrives: there is no way to watch a heap grow and then buy a share of it. An epoch with no stampers burns its rot instead.

Holders

Sorted by what they hold right now. Poke settles an account, forcing the rot it owes into this epoch's heap - anyone may do it to anyone, and if you are stamped this epoch, every sleeper you poke pays you. The column on the right is how much each one is sitting on.

Do something

$ROT has five verbs and no admin. Paste any of these into a terminal with near-cli-rs installed. Nothing here is a special endpoint: these are the whole interface.

Rules

  1. Rot. An unstamped balance loses 2% of itself every day, compounding. ft_balance_of is a function of the clock.
  2. Stamp. stamp() burns 0.5% of your balance, shields the rest for 7 days, and registers your balance as weight in the next epoch.
  3. Compost. Everything that rots goes to the holders who stamped for that epoch, pro rata. If nobody stamped, it burns.
  4. Poke. Rot only moves when an account is settled, and anyone may settle anyone. The economy needs no keeper and no dev.
  5. Claim. Your payout is capped by what you still hold, so stamping a balance and moving it away earns nothing.

No owner. No mint. No pause. No setter of any kind. Every parameter was a constructor argument and is readable above via get_config. The contract account holds no access keys, so not even the deployer can redeploy it. Supply can shrink - stamp costs, rot with no stampers, and unclaimed compost all burn - and can never grow.
